Token creator

HomeWikiChange LogClass CommandsGM ScreenTown builderTreasure plannerCharacter builderNPC builderItem builderEncounter builderMap prepToken creatorPocket trinketsMonster Parts

Turn any picture into a map token with a ring around it: round, square, hex, octagon or triangle. Everything happens in your browser: your picture is never uploaded or stored anywhere, and the finished token is a PNG you download or copy. The only things that ever download are the optional extras you press for (fonts, the animated export's encoder and the AI remove model), and none of them sends your picture anywhere.

  1. Choose a picture, then drag it into place and set its Picture size until the face or figure sits nicely in the token. Zoom the View for close work; that never changes the token itself.
  2. Pick a ring and a shape: painted, metal, carved runes, wood, stone, sci-fi tech or glowing energy, with its thickness and color, or start from a template. A side preset colors it for Player, Ally, Enemy or Neutral, and the locks keep finished parts safe while you work.
  3. Deal with the background: remove it (by color, with Smart select strokes, or with the optional AI remove), or keep the picture whole and Paint backdrop over the parts you don't want. The backdrop can be a color, a gradient or a texture such as parchment, a stone floor or a starfield.
  4. Break the ring so a hat, a horn or a sword tip sits in front of it, and erase pieces of the ring itself if you like.
  5. Add lettering and marks: a name along the ring, a number badge, Elite or Weak, a status pip, or a banner, scroll, plaque, nameplate or neon sign you can drag, turn and resize.
  6. Download the size you need, a numbered batch as one ZIP, an animated loop, or a project file to finish later.

The ring color paints the Painted styles, the lights on the Tech styles and the whole Energy family. Tick Tint every style to shade metal, wood, stone and the rest with it too. Styles marked ▶ move in the animated download and when you press Play. Glow sets how brightly the Energy styles, the neon tube and the lights on the Tech styles shine: 0% leaves them unlit, 200% gives a wide, bright halo. Flicker sets how the neon tube flickers when it animates. Glowing styles look best with a ring size a little under 100%, so the glow has room. The Ring mode erases pieces of the ring (or use Affects: Ring with Erase); erased parts are see-through. A token can have up to three Rings, stacked from the outside in, such as red, white and blue bands: pick a ring in the list to edit it with the style, color, thickness, Glow and Flicker controls, use the arrows to move it out or in, and Gap to space them apart. Ring erasing picks whether the ring eraser works on every ring or just one. The Sculpted family is not a clean band: barbed wire, spikes, thorny vines, a snake biting its tail, two snakes, a dragon, chain, rope, flames, tentacles, bones and crystal shards. Their Length and Count set how far the pieces reach and how many there are; they may reach past the outer edge, so lower the ring size to keep them inside the picture. The snakes, the dragon, the flames, the tentacles and the crystals move a little when animated. Square and hex suit grid and hex maps on other tabletops; the bot's own battle maps show every token as a circle. The lock keeps every ring setting as it is.

Remove background clears the color running in from the picture's edges, so it works best on a plain backdrop. For a busy scene, switch to Cut out and use Smart select: draw a rough Keep stroke over the figure and a Remove stroke over the background, and the edge is found for you. AI remove downloads a small model (about 4.6 MB, plus a one-time engine of about 15 MB from a code CDN) the first time; it runs on this device and your picture never leaves it. Refine edge snaps any cut to the picture's real edges, which helps hair and fur. Then use the Magic wand, Erase and Restore to touch up. Removed parts show faintly while a cut-out tool is on.

Painted backdrop

The backdrop shows wherever the picture is see-through. Instead of removing the background you can keep the whole photo and use the Backdrop mode: Paint backdrop lays the backdrop over the parts you don't want and Unpaint brings the photo back. Nothing is deleted, the picture locks don't stop it, and the painted area moves with the picture. Cover what was removed turns an earlier removal into painted backdrop. The dropper buttons pick a color by tapping the token. The lock keeps the fill, the texture and the painted backdrop as they are.

4Break the ring

Switch to Pop out and paint over the parts that should sit in front of the ring; Push back undoes it. Only the picture shows there, so remove its background first. Zoom the view to paint along a fine edge.

Lettering follows the ring's outline; on a polygon it runs straight along each side. Fonts load from Google Fonts the first time you pick one (offline, a system font stands in). Badges sit on the ring at a clock position. Placed objects are moved with the Objects mode: drag one to move it, the corner handle resizes it and the top handle turns it; arrow keys nudge it and Delete removes it. Props are little holiday stickers and costume pieces: tap one to add it, then drag, size, turn or flip it (the arrow handle at its top-left corner). Hats and antlers sit In front of the ring so they break over it like a pop-out; Behind the ring tucks a prop under the ring and over the picture. Most props take a color. The string of lights hangs Around the ring (drag it to slide it along, Reach sets how far it goes) or Straight; the lights twinkle and the snowflakes drift in Play and the animated download, and a still download shows every light lit. A neon sign has its own Glow (0% is switched off, 200% blazes) and Flicker, which shows in the preview and the animated download. The Objects lock in Layers and locks keeps them all in place.

6Download

Creature size

A PNG with a see-through outside. 512 px suits most maps; the creature sizes give a bigger creature more pixels so it stays sharp when it covers several squares (the bot scales every token to the grid itself, so 512 px is plenty there). Copy image copies the 512 px token so you can paste it straight into a Discord message. The drop shadow lifts the token off the map; leave it off if your map adds its own. To use it in Table Goblin, upload it with /tgchar set_avatar for your character, or /tgnpc set_avatar for a GM's creature. The bot checks every uploaded image for inappropriate content, the same as any other avatar.

Numbered batch
Count

Makes up to 12 copies of this token with the number badge counting up (Goblin 1 to Goblin 6), all in one ZIP at 512 px. It uses the badge's shape, color and spot from section 5.

Animated
Loop

Animates the neon, glowing runes, energy shield, holographic, plasma, ember and HUD reticle rings and flickering neon signs, at the Glow and Flicker you set. APNG keeps soft, see-through edges; GIF works everywhere but its edges are hard. The encoder downloads the first time you use it. The bot's battle maps show the first frame only, since maps are drawn as still pictures; other tabletops and profile pictures can show the animation.

Project file

A project file (.tgtoken.json) holds the picture, its cut-out, pop-out, painted backdrop, ring erasing, lettering, objects and every setting, so you can finish the token later or share it as a template. It is saved only where you put it; nothing is kept by this site or in the browser. A ring preset holds the settings only, without the picture; open it the same way.
